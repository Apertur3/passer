Generative art · style spec · summary

Plotter-tradition pieces: grey paper, black ink, slow loops

Every piece is one deterministic drawing function that renders as a poster, a hero background, a seamless loop video, an avatar or a live article embed. The look comes from the 1960s to 1980s computer-art tradition (Nees, Molnar, Morellet, Mohr, Nake, Csuri, Reas, plus Escher-style tilings): lines and dots only, static paper grain on every piece, no gradient blobs or glow. Motion is slow, loops exactly every 20 seconds by default, and always includes a rest.

Article embeds: one self-contained HTML file (iframe or inline script), sized to its column (700 px, 390 px phones), pauses offscreen, still frame for reduced motion, no network requests, under 30 KB, 3 ms per frame.

Three calls you may want to change

  1. Chrome torus, blurry blob and ink-blot tiles are left out. They need photoreal shading or blur, which drifts toward the gradient look and needs WebGL. Say so if you want one back.
  2. Grain is static, like paper. The alternative is film-style grain that shifts every frame: more "crackle", but it shimmers.
  3. Paper is a warm light grey (#E4E2DD). The reference grid is a cooler grey; a cooler paper would be about #E6E6E4.
